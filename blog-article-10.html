Meniscus Injuries & Arthroscopic Treatment

Updated: January 2025

Ms. (Dr.) Samantha Tross

Consultant Orthopaedic and Trauma Surgeon

First Black Female Orthopaedic Consultant in the UK

The meniscus is a vital shock absorber inside the knee, and when it tears — whether through injury or wear-and-tear — it can cause pain, swelling, and locking sensations. Understanding whether arthroscopy is helpful depends on the type of tear and the condition of the knee overall.

In younger or athletic individuals, tears often occur suddenly during twisting or pivoting movements. These types of tears sometimes benefit from repair, where the surgeon restores the meniscus using sutures. In cases of degenerative tears (often related to arthritis or ageing), repair may not be possible, and symptoms may improve more effectively with physiotherapy or non-surgical management.

Arthroscopy today is far more refined than in the past. Modern techniques focus on preserving as much healthy meniscus as possible, since long-term joint health is improved when the meniscus remains intact. For suitable candidates, arthroscopy can relieve symptoms and restore mobility with smaller incisions and quicker recovery times.

An accurate diagnosis is key — and imaging, examination, and symptom history all guide the treatment plan. With the right approach, most patients regain confidence in their knee and return to their usual activities safely.
