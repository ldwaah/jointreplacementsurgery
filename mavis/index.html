Why women come to me

Nothing is wrong with your life.
That is exactly the problem.

On paper it works. Underneath it, a quiet sense that you have outgrown the life you built — and no language for saying so out loud.

  • 01 You are the one who copes

    Everyone else got to fall apart. You got competent instead — and now nobody thinks to ask how you are.

  • 02 Something happened, and you moved on too fast

    A loss, a diagnosis, a betrayal, a birth. You processed it in the gaps between other people’s needs.

  • 03 The achievement stopped working

    You got the thing. The relief lasted a fortnight. You are already looking for the next thing to prove.

  • 04 You have done the reading

    You can name your patterns beautifully. Insight has become another way of staying in control.

The approach

Where psychology meets
soulful insight

Most therapy asks you to pick a side. I never have.

CBT works. It is the most rigorously evidenced talking therapy there is, and my accreditation with the BABCP means I have been assessed against that standard and am held to it. You will leave sessions with things that actually change your week.

But structure without soul is a worksheet, and I have never believed that the evidence base and the sacred have to be separate rooms. So we will track the thought and we will also sit with the ache underneath it. We will build the skill and we will honour what it cost you to need it.

That is the whole of my practice, in one line: rigorous enough to work, human enough to stay.

The method

How the work actually goes

Therapy, coaching or a course — the shape is the same. You should know what you are walking into before you pay for it.

01

Land

What is true today, not your history. Most women arrive performing wellness out of habit. The first job is to stop.

02

Name

We map what keeps the problem going — thoughts, avoidance, the beliefs underneath. This is where CBT is most precise.

03

Unhook

We separate what was survival from what is still choice, and test new responses in real life. The part that takes courage.

04

Become

Something in place of the old strategy, practised until it holds without me.

The aim is not that you need me forever. It is that you leave with an internal version of this room.

Before you book

The things people ask me

The BABCP is the lead organisation for CBT in the UK. Accreditation means my training, supervised clinical hours and ongoing practice have all been formally assessed against their standard — and that I am held to their code of conduct, with a complaints route that does not go through me. Anyone can call themselves a therapist. Not everyone can be accredited. You can search the public register to confirm it.

The most common question I get, and you do not have to answer it alone. Broadly: therapy tends to distress and to the past that is still shaping the present; coaching builds a future you can already see but cannot reach. We decide together on the free call — and if what you need is therapy, I will not sell you coaching.

Twenty minutes, free, nothing to prepare. You say as much or as little as you want, I ask a few questions, and we work out whether I am the right person. Nobody is sold anything. If I am not the right fit, I will say so and point you somewhere better.

CBT is designed to be time-limited, which is one of its quiet kindnesses. Twelve to twenty sessions is typical, though some things resolve faster and some need longer. We review together every six sessions so that continuing is always a decision you are actively making.

Done badly, yes. Done well it is one of the most respectful things you can offer someone: a clear model of what is keeping the pain going, and real skills to change it. I bring the structure. I also bring warmth, and room for the parts of your story a worksheet has no column for.

Then tell me about it on the call — what happened, and what you needed that you did not get. That information is useful, not awkward. Fit matters more than modality, and a poor fit is not a verdict on you.

Yes. What you say stays between us, with the standard legal and ethical exceptions where there is a serious risk of harm — which I will always discuss with you first wherever it is possible to do so. I work within the BABCP Standards of Conduct and take my clinical work to monthly supervision, anonymously.

Fees are published on the therapy page so you can answer that privately, without a sales call. A small number of reduced-fee places are always held. Ask — it has never once been held against anyone, and I would far rather you asked than disappeared.
